Working across styles · ~5 min

Reading Feedback Across Styles on a Global Team

What you'll be able to do: when blunt or vague feedback causes friction, tell apart a direct/indirect communication-style difference from a difference in intent, and choose a reply that fixes the work without escalating.

The setup

You're a product writer at Lumen Pay. Tomorrow you present the new in-app transaction-dispute flow — the screens that walk a customer through reporting a charge they don't recognise. Last night you shared a first draft in the team channel. This morning, two replies are waiting: one from Sam and one from Alex.

Two replies, two very different tones. Your job: work out what each teammate is really telling you, and respond so the work gets better and the relationship stays intact.

#dispute-flow · first-draft review

  1. S

    Sam

    The dispute flow doesn't work yet. Screen 3 is confusing — customers won't know whether to tap “Report” or “Cancel,” and the wording is far too vague about the timeline. This needs a rework before tomorrow.

  2. A

    Alex

    This is looking great — really nice work! I think it's almost there. Maybe screen 3 could be a tiny bit clearer? Just a small thought, totally up to you. Excited to see it tomorrow!

Your decisions

Debrief

Same issue, opposite wrapping

Two teammates, the same real issue — screen 3 — at opposite ends of the directness scale. The skill wasn't guessing where anyone was from; it was reading how each delivers feedback and responding to the substance:

  • Blunt isn't rude — separate the what from the how.
  • Soft isn't “no problem” — a specific question surfaces the real ask.
  • When a style differs from yours, flex toward it — confirm the work, rather than matching tone or taking it personally.

You never needed anyone's nationality — only their behaviour. Next time feedback lands wrong, ask: is this style, or substance? Usually it's both.
